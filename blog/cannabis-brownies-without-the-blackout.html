Recipe + Real Math

Cannabis Brownies Without the Blackout

A decarb + dosage guide that does the math your other brownie recipe didn't. Because eating an "I'm dying" dose isn't a fun story — it's a long, panicked night.

By Blazin Bill · Published May 6, 2026 · Updated November 28, 2026 (what changed: we swapped the old one-number dose shortcut and the decarb “efficiency” claim for figures from published studies, and every dose on the page now shows a working estimate with a low–high range. Timing, the too-much advice and the box-mix table were rechecked against controlled brownie studies, brand packaging and retailer listings.)

TL;DR: Decarb cannabis flower in a thin layer at 240°F (115°C) for 40 minutes to turn THCA into active THC[1][2]. Make cannabutter or cannaoil. Then do the math: grams × THC% × 10 × (0.576 to 0.863) ÷ pieces = mg per brownie, or about grams × THC% × 10 × 0.75 ÷ pieces for a single number (75%, a round value near the middle of the measured range, not a measurement)[3]. One gram of 20% flower in a 16-piece pan is about 9.4 mg per brownie (7.2–10.8); size your servings from the high end. Health Canada says start at 2.5 mg or less[4]. One piece is the serving this math is sized for. Controlled brownie studies found effects peaked 1.5–3 hours after eating and lasted 6–8[5][6], and Health Canada says full effects can take up to 4 hours[4]; judge the first piece on that clock before deciding on a second. In one study, a high-fat meal raised total exposure and delayed the peak[7]. In the blackout-brownie stories we hear, people didn't wait long enough and didn't do the math.

Why many online brownie recipes skip the dosing math

A person I met at a Cleveland event told me the same story I've heard at cannabis gatherings for years: they made brownies at home, ate one, didn't feel anything, ate two more, and spent the rest of the night convinced they were dying. Hospital adjacent. The friend who'd shown them the recipe ate a quarter of one and said "yeah these came out okay" while they spiraled into panic on the couch.

This is the canonical home-edible story. No lethal dose of THC has been established in the research we checked, but edibles are not harmless[8]. The problem is that many online brownie recipes skip the dosing math entirely. They tell you to use "an ounce of weed" and "cut into 16 pieces" without ever calculating what that means in milligrams of THC per piece. The answer is often way too much.

So here's the recipe with the math. Save yourself, and your friends, the panicked night.

Step 1: Decarbing — what it is, why it matters

Raw cannabis flower contains mostly THCA, with a little THC. THCA is the acidic precursor to THC, and your body doesn't get high from it. To make active THC, you have to apply heat, which knocks the carboxyl group off the molecule (the "decarb" of decarboxylation). Smoking does this automatically. An infusion without a separate oven step can leave a large share of the acid form, so do the decarb deliberately first.

If you skip the decarb step and toss raw flower straight into your butter, you'll lose most of your THC potential. A simmer isn't hot enough to finish the job: pharmacy-made cannabis oils heated at about 212°F (100°C) or more with no oven step first still had 50–90% of their cannabinoids in the inactive acid form[3].

The right temperature and time

The setting with the most evidence behind it: 240°F (115°C) for 40 minutes, in a thin layer. That's the oven step an Italian pharmacy protocol uses before making cannabis oil[2], and in a lab test on a cannabis extract in a vacuum oven, THCA was close to fully converted after 30 minutes at 230°F (110°C)[1]. Our decarb temperature chart has the full time-and-temperature breakdown.

You'll see "240°F converts about 88 percent of THCA" all over the internet, and on the older version of this page. No study we found measured that. It most likely comes from the 0.877 molecular-weight factor that regulators use to convert THCA to THC on a label[9], misread as a conversion rate.

Hotter isn't better. You'll also read that 250°F and up "turns THC into CBN." No study we found measured CBN formation from flower in an oven at 250°F. In the one flower study we found that did measure it, hemp flower sealed in small vials at 446°F (230°C) for 12 minutes ended up with more than 3% CBN in a THC-rich variety[10]. At higher oven temperatures the main loss is THC evaporating: losses have been measured from about 250°F (120°C) up, and they grow with time and temperature[10][11]. Lower temperatures work but take longer: at 203°F (95°C) the reaction wasn't finished after an hour[1].

How to actually do it

  1. Preheat oven to 240°F (115°C). Use an oven thermometer if yours runs hot.
  2. Weigh the flower before you decarb it. That's the number your dose math uses.
  3. Break flower into small pieces or grind it coarse. One 2026 study using finely ground flower in a thin bed measured rates about twice those reported elsewhere, and its authors suggest the grind and bed depth may be why[10].
  4. Spread in a thin layer on a parchment-lined baking sheet: no more than 5 mm deep, ideally 1–2 mm[2]. Don't pile it.
  5. Bake for 40 minutes. Don't keep opening the oven; every peek drops the temperature.
  6. Look for: light golden-brown color (not dark brown). The kitchen will smell like cannabis. That's normal.
  7. Let it cool. The flower is now ready for infusion.

Pro tip: a foil pouch sealed with a fold can cut the kitchen smell. No study has tested whether a sealed pouch changes the decarb; the thin-layer method above is the one the pharmacy protocol uses.

Can you use THCA flower for brownies?

Yes. THCA is most of what's in raw flower, and it's exactly what the decarb step turns into THC. Ohio and Michigan labs already do the conversion on the label: Total THC = THC + 0.877 × THCA (both also count other THC isomers, such as delta-8). Ohio's own example: 0.20% THC + 0.11% delta-8 + 0.877 × 22.70% THCA = 20.22% Total THC[9][12]. Hemp-derived "THCA flower" follows the same math: decarb it the same way, use the Total THC number from its lab certificate, and if the certificate lists only THCA, multiply that by 0.877 and add any THC. Florida readers: we couldn't confirm which formula Florida labels use, so check whether yours says "Total THC."

Already-vaped bud (AVB): it's already decarbed, but nobody has measured how much THC is left after a real home session. In one lab vaporizer test, flower that started at 14.5% total THC tested 7.10% after three minutes at 356°F (180°C)[13]. Treat AVB as low and unknown potency, and don't plug a label number into the math for it.

Step 2: Pick your path — boxed mix or scratch

Most home cannabis bakers reach for a boxed mix. That's totally fine, and a box is easier to size because the yield and fat are printed on the box. Your job is just to substitute cannabutter for the oil/butter the box calls for, then cut into the recommended number of pieces.

Whether you go boxed or scratch, here's the rule: your dose math depends entirely on (a) how much cannabis you put in your fat, and (b) how many pieces you cut at the end. Different boxes yield different numbers of brownies — that's the variable that bites people who don't read the box.

Betty Crocker weed brownies (box-mix method)

The current Betty Crocker Fudge Brownie Mix (16.3 oz) calls for 1/2 cup vegetable oil, 2 tablespoons water and 2 eggs (3 eggs for cake-like), and makes 16 servings[15]. Here's the swap:

  1. Decarb 1 g of flower (Step 1) and make 1/2 cup cannabutter with it (below).
  2. Melt the cannabutter gently and use it in place of the 1/2 cup oil, with the box's water and eggs.
  3. Bake per the box: 13×9 pan at 350°F for 21–24 minutes, 9×9 at 350°F for 33–36 minutes, or 8×8 at 325°F for 49–52 minutes; nonstick pans need about 3 minutes less[15].
  4. Cool completely, then cut exactly 16.

With 15% flower that's about 7.0 mg per brownie (5.4–8.1). With 20% flower it's about 9.4 mg (7.2–10.8), and the high end tops the 10 mg adult-use per-serving cap in Ohio and Michigan[21][22]. As arithmetic under this model's high estimate, about 0.9 g of 20% flower, or 20 pieces (7.5 mg each, 5.8–8.6), keeps each piece under 10 mg. That 10 mg is a commercial serving cap, not a personal safety line.

Step 2 (continued): Make cannabutter sized to your box

Make exactly the volume the box calls for. Don't pre-make a giant batch and use part of it — that creates mental math nobody does correctly the first time. Match cannabutter volume to box fat volume.

Cannabutter recipe — scaled to box

The sizing rule: the butter matches the fat on your box, with an equal amount of water to keep it from scorching. The flower matches the dose you want and the number of pieces you'll cut. Because all of the butter goes into one pan, the butter volume doesn't change the per-brownie dose; only the grams and the cut count do.

  • For a 1/2 cup box (Betty Crocker Fudge; King Arthur cake-like, 1 stick): 1 g flower + 1/2 cup butter + 1/2 cup water
  • For a 1/3 cup box (King Arthur fudgy): 1 g flower + 1/3 cup butter + 1/3 cup water
  • For scratch (1 cup, below): 1 g flower + 1 cup butter + 1 cup water
  • Any other box: match the fat printed on your box, and pick the flower from the table in Step 3 using your box's cut count.

Each of those, cut into 16, comes out around 7.0 mg per brownie (5.4–8.1) with 15% flower or 9.4 mg (7.2–10.8) with 20% flower. Plan around the high number.

Method (same for all sizes):

  1. Combine butter, water, and decarbed flower in a small saucepan over the lowest heat your stove offers. Hold it between 160°F and 212°F (70–100°C). Small bubbles are fine; keep it from a rolling, splattering boil.
  2. Simmer gently for 30 to 60 minutes, stirring now and then. Longer isn't stronger: in pharmacy oil tests, 30, 60 and 120 minutes at 212°F pulled out about the same amount of THC[2].
  3. Strain through cheesecloth into a heat-safe container. Press gently.
  4. Refrigerate at least 2 hours. Butter solidifies on top; water sinks below. Discard water; keep butter.
  5. Label it with the date and the strength. Nobody has studied cannabutter shelf life directly; university extension guidance for plain butter is 1–2 months in the fridge and 6–9 months in the freezer, wrapped tightly[18].

Why the water? Water can't get hotter than 212°F while it's boiling, so it puts a ceiling on the pot, the same 100°C that pharmacy procedures use for infusing after an oven decarb[2]. It also keeps the milk solids in butter from scorching on the bottom of the pan. For mg per tablespoon and other ratios, see the cannabutter ratio calculator.

Oil instead of cannabutter (the no-dairy path)

No dairy, or searching for a weed brownie recipe with no cannabutter? Use coconut oil or olive oil. The method is the same: decarb in the oven, hold the oil in the 160–212°F window (a double boiler works; the pharmacy procedures use a boiling-water bath[2]), strain, and store. The math is the same too, and the 57.6–86.3% infusion range this page uses was actually measured in olive oil[3]. In a 90-day study, olive oil and MCT oil held their cannabinoid levels equally well, with MCT pulling more terpenes[19]. Coconut oil hasn't been compared in any study we found, so treat it like any other fat. Oil-based box mixes take cannabis oil straight in place of the oil.

Step 3: The dosage math (this is the part that matters)

Here's the math many recipes skip. It gives you an estimate with a range, not a lab result. Equal portions assume the butter mixed evenly, which no homemade-edible study has measured.

The formula

Total mg THC in batch ≈ grams of flower × label THC% × 10 × (0.576 to 0.863)

Where 0.576–0.863 comes from. In an analysis of 4,774 pharmacy-made cannabis olive oils, average THC recovery for different cannabis varieties and preparation methods ranged from 57.6% to 86.3% of the flower's declared THC content[3]. The oils were grouped by four preparation methods: two (A and B) heated raw flower in oil with no oven step, and two (C and D) decarbed the flower in an oven first. Because recovery is measured against the declared content of the flower, that range already includes losses during heating and extraction. Don't multiply by a separate decarb factor. Nobody has lab-tested home butter at that scale. The 0.75 below is 75%, a round value near the middle of the measured range, not a measurement. The single shortcut factor on the older version of this page has no source we could find[20].

Single number: total mg ≈ grams × THC% × 10 × 0.75 mg per brownie = Total mg THC ÷ number of brownies

Use the Total THC line on your label. In Ohio and Michigan it already includes the 0.877 THCA conversion, so don't apply it again[9][12]. Size your servings from the high end of the range: underestimate your butter and every brownie is stronger than you planned. For the interactive version, use the edible dosage calculator.

Weed brownie recipe with 1 gram of flower: how strong is it?

One gram is a common starting batch. Decarbed, infused and baked into a 16-piece pan, 1 gram of flower comes out to:

  • 15% THC flower: about 113 mg in the batch (86–129), or 7.0 mg per brownie (5.4–8.1)
  • 20% THC flower: about 150 mg in the batch (115–173), or 9.4 mg per brownie (7.2–10.8)
  • 25% THC flower: about 188 mg in the batch (144–216), or 11.7 mg per brownie (9.0–13.5)

For reference, 10 mg is the adult-use per-serving cap in Ohio and Michigan and Florida's medical cap; Ohio medical allows 55 mg and Michigan medical 50 mg per serving[21][22][23]. Homemade brownies aren't regulated, and that cap is a rule for commercial servings, not a personal safety line, but it is a useful yardstick. A gram of 15% flower in 16 pieces stays under it even at this model's high estimate (8.1 mg). A gram of 20% flower can top it (10.8 mg at the high end). As arithmetic under the high estimate, about 0.9 g or 20 pieces (about 7.5 mg each, 5.8–8.6) keeps each piece under 10 mg; 24 pieces drops the 20% batch to about 6.3 mg each (4.8–7.2).

Worked examples — by box

Example 1: Betty Crocker Fudge (16 brownies, 1/2 cup oil). Make 1/2 cup cannabutter using 1g of 15% THC flower. Substitute for the oil. Cut into 16.

1 × 15 × 10 × 0.75 = ~113 mg THC total (86–129)
113 ÷ 16 = ~7.0 mg per brownie (5.4–8.1) ✓

Example 2: The same batch, cut into 24. Same 1g of 15% THC flower, same box, but cut in a 6×4 grid instead of 4×4.

1 × 15 × 10 × 0.75 = ~113 mg THC total (86–129)
113 ÷ 24 = ~4.7 mg per brownie (3.6–5.4) ✓ (gentler)

Same cannabis input. Different cut. Different dose. This is exactly why you have to know your piece count before doing the math.

Example 3: King Arthur All-American Fudge, cake-like (16 brownies, 1 stick butter) — heavier batch. 2g of 18% THC flower in 1/2 cup cannabutter, cut into 16.

2 × 18 × 10 × 0.75 = ~270 mg THC total (207–311)
270 ÷ 16 = ~16.9 mg per brownie (13.0–19.4)

That's over the 10 mg adult-use per-serving cap in Ohio and Michigan[21][22]. Half a brownie works out to about 8.4 mg (6.5–9.7), under 10 mg at this model's high estimate.

Example 4: The "blackout brownie" pattern. Recipe says "use an ounce" of 20% THC flower. 28g goes into a Betty Crocker box → 16 brownies:

28 × 20 × 10 × 0.75 = ~4,200 mg THC total (3,226–4,833)
4,200 ÷ 16 = ~263 mg per brownie (202–302) 💀

That's about 26 times a 10 mg serving (20–30 times). In a controlled study, 25 mg and 50 mg brownies already "markedly impaired" people who didn't use cannabis regularly[5]. This is how a blackout brownie happens. If a recipe calls for an ounce of flower in a single box of brownies, walk away.

Why the cut count itself is a dosing tool

Want a gentler per-piece dose without changing your cannabis input? Cut more pieces. The box's serving count is a suggestion, not a rule.

Same cannabis input (1g of 15% THC flower ≈ 113 mg, range 86–129), spread across:

  • 16 brownies: 7.0 mg each (5.4–8.1)
  • 20 brownies: 5.6 mg each (4.3–6.5)
  • 24 brownies: 4.7 mg each (3.6–5.4)
  • 32 small squares: 3.5 mg each (2.7–4.0)

For Health Canada's "2.5 mg or less" starting point[4], halve the flower: 0.5 g of 15% flower cut into 32 squares lands around 1.8 mg each (1.4–2.0).

Step 4: Why edibles hit so much harder than smoking

Smoke or vape and the THC in your blood peaks within about 30 minutes[25]. Eat the same plant and the clock changes: in controlled brownie studies, effects didn't show up until 30–60 minutes after eating, peaked at 1.5–3 hours and lasted 6–8 hours[5][6]. Part of the reason is 11-hydroxy-THC.

When you eat THC, your gut wall and liver metabolize much of it before it reaches your bloodstream[26]. Only 6% (± 3%) of THC eaten in a chocolate cookie reached the blood in one classic study, against 18% (± 6%) smoked[27]. A large share of what gets metabolized becomes 11-hydroxy-THC. After a 20 mg THC brownie, total blood exposure to 11-OH-THC was about 1.2 times that of THC itself[28]. A 2026 preprint, not yet peer reviewed, that pooled 112 people from seven controlled trials found a bigger share of 11-OH-THC after eating than after inhaling, and estimated 11-OH-THC at about 1.4 times as potent as THC per molecule for the subjective high, but with a lower ceiling[29].

Translation: the same milligrams by mouth and by inhalation do not give the same exposure or effect, so don't use your smoking experience to size an edible. The longer explanation, plus a serving calculator, is on our edible dosage calculator page.

And if you're buying rather than baking, that "10mg" on the package has its own credibility problem. In a 2015 study of 75 edible products bought in San Francisco, Los Angeles and Seattle, only 17% had THC within ±10% of the label[34]. Those were medical products from before today's state testing rules, so they don't tell you how accurate current Ohio products are. See Dosage & Effect Drift in Commercial Gummies for the lab data on why labeled doses and actual doses so often disagree.

The peak window: why the first piece needs hours

The classic over-consumption story always runs the same way: eat one edible, feel nothing yet, eat two more, then have all three arrive together. The researchers behind one of those brownie studies put it plainly: the delayed onset "increases the chances of acute overdose"[5].

In controlled studies, effects started 30–60 minutes after eating and peaked at 1.5–3 hours[5], and lasted 6–8 hours[6]. Health Canada says it can take up to 4 hours to feel the full effects[4], and the CDC says 30 minutes to 2 hours just to feel anything[30]. Food matters too: in one study, a high-fat meal raised total exposure and delayed the peak[7]. One piece is the serving this math is sized for. Controlled brownie studies found effects peaked 1.5–3 hours after eating, and Health Canada says full effects can take up to 4 hours; judge the first piece on that clock before deciding on a second. Set a timer. Don't trust your own patience.

Step 5a: Boxed mix workflow (recommended for most bakers)

Box + Cannabutter Method

Yields: Whatever the box says. Active time: decarb 40 min + cannabutter 30–60 min + bake per box, plus at least 2 hours for the butter to set in the fridge.

  1. Decarb your flower (Step 1 above).
  2. Make cannabutter at a volume that matches your box's fat requirement (Step 2 above).
  3. Open the box. Read the back panel: how much oil does it call for? How many servings? What pan?
  4. Melt your cannabutter gently. Substitute for the oil 1:1 by volume.
  5. Mix the box per directions (cannabutter + water + eggs + mix). Bake per box directions.
  6. Cool completely before cutting.
  7. Cut into the box's recommended servings, or more for a gentler dose — count precisely. Use a ruler.
  8. Label the storage container with date + mg per brownie + total brownie count. Store it in the fridge or freezer, out of reach of kids and pets.

Step 5b: Scratch recipe (16 brownies, for the curious)

If you'd rather skip the box, here's a scratch recipe sized to yield 16 brownies — the same count both verified box mixes print — for apples-to-apples dosing math. Baking gluten-free? King Arthur's Gluten-Free Ultimate Fudge box takes 1/2 cup melted butter and makes 16, so the box method above works with it[31].

Scratch Cannabis Brownies (16 servings)

Yields: 16 brownies in a 9×13 pan. Time: about 4½ hours start to finish: about 1½ hours of prep (decarb 40 min, then cannabutter with a 30–60 min simmer), at least 2 hours for the butter to set in the fridge, and 25–30 min in the oven.

Ingredients:

  • 1 cup cannabutter (made above with 1 g of flower, or the amount you calculated)
  • 3/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder
  • 1 cup white sugar
  • 1 cup brown sugar (packed)
  • 4 large eggs
  • 1 tablespoon vanilla extract
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 cup chocolate chips (optional but encouraged)

Instructions:

  1. Preheat oven to 350°F. Grease a 9×13 baking pan.
  2. Melt cannabutter gently — microwave 30 seconds at a time, stirring. Don't let it bubble hard or brown.
  3. Whisk melted cannabutter, cocoa, and both sugars until smooth.
  4. Beat in eggs one at a time, then vanilla.
  5. Stir in flour, salt, and baking powder. Mix just until combined — overmixing makes brownies cake-like.
  6. Fold in chocolate chips.
  7. Pour into pan. Bake 25-30 minutes until a toothpick comes out with moist crumbs (not dry).
  8. Cool completely before cutting.
  9. Cut 16 even squares in a 4×4 grid. Use a ruler. Equal portions assume the butter mixed evenly, which no homemade-edible study has measured.

A kitchen scale that reads to 0.1 g helps.

If someone takes too much (yours or theirs)

It happens. Even with the math, someone underestimates onset and doubles up. If you or a friend is having a too-much experience:

  1. Take it seriously, but breathe. No lethal dose of THC has been established in the research we checked, but edibles are not harmless. At one Colorado hospital, edibles accounted for 10.7% of cannabis-attributable ER visits in 2014–2016, while edibles were only 0.32% of the THC sold in Colorado. Edible visits were more often for intoxication, psychiatric symptoms and heart symptoms than visits after inhaled cannabis[8].
  2. Find a quiet, comfortable space. Lights low. TV muted or off. Stay with someone sober, and nobody drives.
  3. Drink water.
  4. Don't count on CBD. One controlled study gave 640 mg CBD together with 20 mg THC in a brownie and found the effects stronger, not weaker: more anxiety, sedation, heart rate and impairment[28]. It does not test CBD as a rescue after the fact.
  5. Black pepper, chewed or smelled, is a hypothesis from one 2011 paper, with no human trial behind it[32]. If the smell calms you, fine; don't count on it.
  6. Get emergency help (911) for: chest pain, fainting, trouble breathing, a seizure, someone who won't respond, severe confusion, or thoughts of self-harm. For anything else you're unsure about, call poison control at 1-800-222-1222; the CDC's advice is to call poison control, or 911 in an emergency[30].
  7. In controlled studies, effects peaked 1.5–3 hours after eating and lasted 6–8 hours[5][6]. Don't take more.

Lock them up. Reported edible exposures in children under 6 rose from 207 in 2017 to 3,054 in 2021, almost all at home, and 22.7% of those kids were admitted to the hospital[33]. Label the container, store it out of reach, and keep it away from the regular snacks. If a child eats one, call poison control at 1-800-222-1222 right away; the CDC says children can become very sick[30].

The senior-specific note

Health Canada's general advice is to look for 2.5 mg of THC or less[4]. A 2021 expert consensus on medical cannabis for chronic pain, which is about frailty and sensitivity rather than age, started THC at 2.5 mg in its routine protocol and at 1 mg in a conservative protocol for frail patients, people on several medications, or those more sensitive to drug effects[24]. We found no edible study done specifically in older adults. Whatever number you're aiming for, size it from the high end of the range, so even the high estimate doesn't go over it. Anyone with a heart condition or on prescription medications should talk with a clinician first. For a deeper read, see our sister site Weed: A Senior's Guide, which covers cannabis dosing and consumption methods for adults 50+.

The bottom line

Cannabis brownies are not hard to make. They're just easy to overdose on if you skip the math. The decarb step converts most of the THCA. The infusion pulls most of it into the fat, within a range measured in pharmacy olive oils. The dosage formula gives you an estimated range; the high end is an estimate, not a guaranteed maximum. One piece is the serving this math is sized for. Controlled brownie studies found effects peaked 1.5–3 hours after eating, and Health Canada says full effects can take up to 4 hours; judge the first piece on that clock before deciding on a second.

That's it. Bake. Wait. Enjoy.
